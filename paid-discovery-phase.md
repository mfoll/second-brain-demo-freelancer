# Paid Discovery Phase

A small, fixed-fee first engagement that answers what the problem actually is before a client commits to the full project.

## How it worked at Copperline

Copperline started with discovery only: $18,500 for interviews, a baseline test and a report ([[copperline-discovery-research]]). The report then justified a $62,000 phase 2 ([[copperline-statement-of-work]]). Without the baseline, I would have been pricing a redesign of something nobody had measured.

## Why it matters now

Velora chose Orchard & Vane partly because they offered a $38,000 discovery phase first while I offered one $74,000 price ([[velora-feedback-call-2026-06-03]]). A paid first step is less risky for the buyer and gives them an easy yes. Since the [[proposal-template-rework]] every project proposal over 10 days leads with a paid discovery or diagnostic option.

## Limits

- Small projects do not need it; for an 8-day audit the audit is the discovery.
- Discovery that ends with "we need more research" annoys clients. It should end with a decision and a priced next step.
- The client may take the report to someone cheaper. That has not happened yet; if it does, at least the discovery was paid.

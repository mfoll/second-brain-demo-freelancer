# Usability testing

## Summary

Overview of usability testing: evaluating a product by watching representative users try real tasks, as distinct from inspection methods where experts judge the interface. It covers what tests measure (efficiency, accuracy, recall, emotional response), common setups including remote and hallway testing, and the long argument about how many participants are enough.

## Why It Matters

Pre-reading for the first Marrowgate class. The section on sample size is why I wrote down a policy in [[how-many-participants-is-enough-for-a-usability-test]]: the famous "five users" figure is about finding problems, not measuring them. I also use it as background for [[unmoderated-testing]], which the article treats under remote testing.

## Caveats

General and uneven; parts read like a list of methods. Nothing here on accessibility testing with assistive technology.

# Write A Three-Option Proposal

How I turn a qualified lead into a proposal, using [[proposal-template-2026]]. For work over 10 days; for shorter work use the day-rate variant and skip steps 3 and 4.

## Inputs

- Notes from the discovery call ([[qualify-a-new-lead]]): the problem, what it costs, what a good result is worth, who decides, how they compare suppliers.
- [[capacity-calendar-2026]] and partner availability ([[partner-availability-agreements-2026]]).
- Approved outcomes from [[testimonial-quotes-collection]].

## Steps

1. Check the pricing rule ([[pricing-rules-2026]]). If the client compares on day rates or the work is under 10 days, use the short variant.
2. Write the problem in the client's words, half a page. Send it to the client for a "yes, that's it" before pricing if the scope is fuzzy.
3. Draft options: A a paid first step ([[paid-discovery-phase]]), B what I recommend, C B plus one valuable extra. Each with scope, exclusions, timeline.
4. Price each option from the value, then check it against the floor with a 30% overrun ([[pricing-floor-calculation]], [[effective-day-rate]]). If C is a decoy nobody should buy, rewrite it ([[wikipedia-decoy-effect]]).
5. Confirm partner availability by email before naming anyone.
6. Fill the outcomes page with numbers relevant to this client.
7. Read it as the client's finance person: price page first. Then as procurement: is capacity answered?
8. Send as PDF with a short email; offer a 20-minute call to walk through options. Log in the [[client-pipeline-tracker]].

## Verification

- Three options with different scope, not discounts.
- No unapproved client numbers.
- Terms point to the [[standard-services-agreement]].
- Follow up after 5 working days if no answer, once more after 10, then mark as quiet.

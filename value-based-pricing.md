# Value-Based Pricing

Setting a price from what the result is worth to the client, not from how many days the work will take me. In my practice it always comes with options, and it has a lower bound: the work below about 10 days where it costs more effort than it earns.

## Definition

> [!info]
> Value-based pricing means estimating what the outcome is worth to this client, then pricing a share of that value, offered as a small set of options so the client chooses the scope. My hours become a private check against a floor, not the basis of the price.

The general idea, as [[wikipedia-value-based-pricing]] describes it, is pricing by perceived value and willingness to pay rather than by cost. For a design practice, the "value" is usually one of: fewer support calls, more conversions or completed sign-ups, lower compliance risk, or a faster developer handoff.

## How I apply it

1. In the discovery call, ask what the problem costs now and what a good result is worth ([[qualify-a-new-lead]]). Ledgerleaf could tell me what a trial user who sends a first invoice is worth to them; Copperline could tell me what a call to member services costs.
2. Write three options with different scope, not three prices for the same scope ([[three-option-proposals]], [[write-a-three-option-proposal]]).
3. Check each option against my floor ([[pricing-floor-calculation]]) and against the [[effective-day-rate]] I would earn if it ran 30% over.
4. Offer a paid first step as option A ([[paid-discovery-phase]]).

## Where it works

- Projects over about 10 days with a measurable outcome. Ledgerleaf ($24,000 middle option, [[ledgerleaf-proposal-2025-12]]) and Copperline phase 2 ($62,000 middle option, [[copperline-statement-of-work]]) both chose the middle option without negotiating.
- Clients who can name what the problem costs them.

## Its limits

- **Small fixed-scope work.** An audit's value to the client is a fix list. There is no revenue figure to anchor on, and buyers compare suppliers on day rates. Two audit leads went quiet after receiving options in spring 2026; Tallowmere asked for a day rate outright. Since [[pricing-check-in-2026-06-18]], work under 10 days is quoted at $720 a day ([[day-rates-suit-small-fixed-work]]).
- **The client must believe the value.** If I can't show outcomes from past work, the price looks arbitrary. That was part of the Velora problem ([[velora-feedback-call-2026-06-03]]).
- **Discovery cost.** Working out the value takes a call and a day of thinking. Unbillable for small jobs.
- **Anchoring cuts both ways.** Three options anchor the client on the middle one, which is the point ([[wikipedia-anchoring-effect]]). But a single high number anchors them against me, as $74,000 did with Velora.

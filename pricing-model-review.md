# Pricing Model Review

Six weeks in autumn 2025 to work out why my first year of freelancing felt busy and earned less than it should, and to replace "days times rate" with something written down. It ended with the decision of 2025-11-20 to stop quoting day rates and send three-option value-based proposals for all work. That decision was partly reversed on 2026-06-18.

## Outcome

A written pricing model replacing day rates with value-based three-option proposals. Reached on 2025-11-20 ([[pricing-model-2025]], [[pricing-decision-2025-11-20]]). Partly superseded by [[pricing-check-in-2026-06-18]], which brought back a $720 day rate for audits, workshops and work under 10 days.

## Current State

- Completed. The 2025 model is kept for history; the rule I apply today is in [[pricing-rules-2026]] and [[services-menu-2026]].
- What still holds: three options for anything over 10 days, a paid first step, and pricing from the client's outcome rather than from my hours.
- What did not hold: "no day rates at all". Audit buyers compare day rates, and an options proposal for an 8-day audit is overkill ([[day-rates-suit-small-fixed-work]]).

## Scope and Boundaries

How I price and how I write the price into a proposal. Not contract terms (deposit, net 15, late fee), which came out of the separate [[contract-terms-update]] in June 2025. Not Lintel Kit pricing.

## Working Context

The trigger was [[crumbhollow-preorder-flow]]. I quoted 18 days at $650, $11,700, and it took 27 days because the shop owners kept adding pickup rules and I absorbed them. Effective rate: about $433 a day ([[crumbhollow-time-log]]). When I laid out every 2025 engagement in the [[effective-day-rate-sheet]], Crumbhollow was the worst but not the only one: only the Orchard & Vane overflow contract, a flat fee that worked out at about $730 a day, came out above the rate I thought I was charging.

Inputs over the six weeks:

- Lunch with [[yvonne-takahashi]] on 2025-10-16 ([[yvonne-takahashi-pricing-lunch-2025-10-16]]): "Price the thing they get, not the time you spend." She also warned me to keep a floor.
- Reading on [[value-based-pricing]] and anchoring ([[wikipedia-value-based-pricing]], [[wikipedia-anchoring-effect]]).
- The Solo Studio Circle session on 2025-11-20, where I presented the sheet. [[rosalind-ng]] was the one skeptic: she kept day rates for content audits and said she would keep them. I noted it and went ahead anyway; seven months later she turned out to be right for that kind of work.

Options I considered and rejected:

- Raising the day rate to $800 and keeping day-based quotes. Rejected because the overrun problem is about scope, not the rate.
- Hourly billing. Rejected: it rewards slowness and invites line-item arguments.
- Retainers only. Rejected for now: most of my clients buy projects, not ongoing hours.

The first proposal under the new model was [[ledgerleaf-proposal-2025-12]] (three options, middle one accepted at $24,000). The Copperline phase 2 proposal in December followed the same shape ([[copperline-statement-of-work]]).

# Capacity Signalling

How a one-person practice shows a buyer that the work will get done even if something goes wrong. Buyers of larger projects score "team and capacity" whether or not the RFP says so; a solo designer starts that score low.

## What counts as a signal

- **Named partners** with roles, rates and written availability, not "I'll bring in help if needed" ([[partner-availability-agreements-2026]]).
- **Booked weeks shown honestly**: the proposal says which weeks I have and at what share of my time ([[capacity-calendar-2026]]).
- **A continuity plan**: who holds the files, how the client gets them if I'm out for two weeks. Every deliverable lives in the client's workspace anyway.
- **Past evidence of sustained work**: Copperline has run for over a year with one designer.

## Where it came from

Velora's third reason ([[velora-feedback-call-2026-06-03]]). Rashid's question about week six was fair, and the answer is a structure, not reassurance. The "Who works on this" page in the [[proposal-template-2026]] is the result.

## Limit

It doesn't make me an agency. For a project that truly needs four people full time, the honest signal is to decline or partner as a sub ([[should-i-answer-cold-rfps]]).

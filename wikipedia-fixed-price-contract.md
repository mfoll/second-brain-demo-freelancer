# Fixed-price contract

## Summary

A fixed-price contract sets a payment that is not adjusted for the resources, costs or time the contractor actually uses. The article contrasts it with cost-plus, time-and-materials and labor-hour contracts, and notes that it puts the cost risk on the contractor.

## Why It Matters

Read right after the [[pricing-check-in-2026-06-18]] while writing [[pricing-rules-2026]]. All my work is fixed-price in this sense, including day-rate audits, which are a fixed number of days, not open-ended time and materials. The risk sits with me, which is exactly what Crumbhollow showed: 27 days on an 18-day price ([[crumbhollow-time-log]], [[effective-day-rate]]).

## Key Details

- Fixed price only works with a well-defined scope; otherwise the contractor absorbs the overrun or argues about change.
- Time-and-materials moves the risk to the client. I don't offer it.

## Caveats

Mostly about government and construction contracting. Contract terms themselves live in the [[standard-services-agreement]].

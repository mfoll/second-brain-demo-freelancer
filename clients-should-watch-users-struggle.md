# Clients Should Watch Users Struggle

## Stance

The decision makers on a client team should watch at least two research sessions live. Seeing a member fail settles arguments that reports never do.

## Scope

Moderated interviews and usability tests where the client can observe from another room or a video link without the participant feeling watched by a crowd. Not for sensitive topics, and never more than three observers.

## Why I Think This

- Renata Solberg watched P7 give up on a transfer in discovery and P3 succeed in the prototype test. The phase 2 budget and the change order both moved faster after those sessions ([[copperline-usability-test-2026-05-21]]).
- Gideon Marsh became an ally on contrast after hearing older members say they could not read the buttons.
- At Thistlewood, Odette Fairweather sat in on all five patron sessions and then argued for the fixes with her own board.

## What Would Change My Mind

Observers who treat sessions as entertainment, or a participant who is noticeably affected by being observed. Either would mean fewer observers and clips instead.

## Implications

Every research plan names observers and books them before recruitment starts. Observer rules (no talking, questions on sticky notes) go in the test plan ([[copperline-prototype-test-plan]]).

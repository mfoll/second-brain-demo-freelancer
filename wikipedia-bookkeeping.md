# Bookkeeping

## Summary

Explains bookkeeping: recording a business's financial transactions day to day so that accurate financial reports can be produced. It covers single-entry and double-entry systems, daybooks and ledgers, and the line between bookkeeping and accounting.

## Why It Matters

Read when I moved from a spreadsheet to Paperbird in March 2025 ([[paperbird-invoicing-setup]]). It made me separate two jobs: recording every transaction promptly (mine, now partly [[paloma-ruiz]]'s) and interpreting them (Delphine's). The habit of matching receipts monthly came from there and is why the 2025 return took one afternoon of cleanup ([[tax-year-2025-filing]]).

## Key Details

- Bookkeeping records transactions; accounting interprets and reports them.
- Single entry records each transaction once, in a cash book much like a checkbook register; double entry changes at least two ledger accounts per transaction.
- Daybooks are chronological, diary-like records; entries are posted to ledgers that feed the financial statements.
- Caveat: general article; says nothing about which system a sole proprietor must keep.

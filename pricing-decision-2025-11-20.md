# Pricing Decision 2025-11-20

The evening I decided to stop quoting day rates. After presenting my 2025 numbers at the November Solo Studio Circle session at Tidewell Commons, I wrote down the rule that every new piece of work gets a value-based proposal with three options. It closed the [[pricing-model-review]].

## At a Glance

> [!note]
> Decision: no more day rates for any work from 2025-11-20. Every proposal gets three options priced on the value to the client, with my day-rate floor used only as a private check. Partly reversed on 2026-06-18 for audits, workshops and work under 10 days.

## Context

The trigger was [[crumbhollow-preorder-flow]]: 18 days quoted at $650, $11,700 in total, 27 days worked. That is about $433 a day ([[crumbhollow-time-log]], [[effective-day-rate]]). The [[effective-day-rate-sheet]] showed the same pattern, less extreme, on Thistlewood. My conclusion in October was that day-based quotes put all the scope risk on me and give the client nothing to choose between.

## What happened at the session

Six of us at the circle, [[rosalind-ng]] chairing. I had 15 minutes and showed the sheet and a draft of [[pricing-model-2025]].

- [[nadia-ferro]] and [[tomas-echeverria]] already priced by project and backed the switch. Tomás said clients pick the middle option about two times in three.
- [[imani-clarke]] asked what I would do when a client asks "what's your day rate?". My answer then: give the floor privately, never in a proposal.
- Rosalind disagreed for one kind of work. She sells content audits at a day rate because buyers compare suppliers line by line, and an options proposal for a two-week audit "looks like you're hiding the price". I noted it as a risk and did not change the rule.

I walked home and wrote the decision into the model the same night.

## Decision

- No day rates in proposals or on the site, for any work.
- Every proposal gets three options ([[three-option-proposals]]). The middle one is the one I recommend.
- Options are priced from what the outcome is worth to the client (reduced support calls, conversions, compliance risk), checked against a private floor of $650 a day ([[pricing-floor-calculation]]).
- Change requests are priced as a new option, never absorbed.

## Rejected options

- Raise the day rate to $800 and keep day quotes.
- Hourly billing.
- Retainers only ([[should-i-offer-retainers]]).

## Consequences

- First proposals under the rule: Ledgerleaf in December 2025 ($24,000 middle option, accepted on 2025-12-08, [[ledgerleaf-proposal-2025-12]]) and Copperline phase 2 ($62,000 middle option, signed on 2025-12-15, [[copperline-statement-of-work]]). Both worked.
- It failed twice in 2026: two audit leads in March and April went quiet after receiving options, and Velora got one big price instead of options because I thought an RFP did not need them ([[velora-pitch]]).
- [[pricing-check-in-2026-06-18]] partly reversed it, and the archived question [[should-i-drop-day-rates-for-all-work]] records the original framing.

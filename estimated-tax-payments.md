# Estimated Tax Payments

Paying income tax during the year in instalments based on expected income, instead of all at once when the return is filed. Self-employed people pay this way because nobody withholds tax from their income.

## How it works for me, in general terms

- The fictional Revenue Office expects four payments a year (15 April, 15 June, 15 September, 15 January). Each is a quarter of the tax on projected income.
- The annual return, due 15 April, settles the difference: pay the balance, or get the overpayment back.
- Underestimating means a balance at filing time; the size of the 2025 one ($3,140) came from income that arrived after the projection was set ([[tax-return-2025-summary]]).

## Estimates versus set-aside

They are different tools. The estimate is what I promise to pay; the set-aside is what I keep so I can pay it and the balance ([[set-aside-taxes-from-each-payment]]). An honest projection keeps the first right; a generous percentage keeps the second safe.

This note describes my own routine and avoids any real country's rules.

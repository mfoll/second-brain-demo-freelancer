# Software license

## Summary

The article on software licenses: legal instruments that govern how software may be used or redistributed, from proprietary end-user licenses to free and open-source ones, and the difference between owning a copy and holding a license to use it.

## Why It Matters

Background for [[lintel-kit-license-terms]] in September 2025. A design kit is not software in the usual sense, but the coded tokens are, and buyers think of the kit in software terms: seats, updates, redistribution. The distinction between buying a copy and buying a license is what makes per-seat terms coherent ([[per-seat-licensing]]), and it is the frame for the [[should-lintel-kit-get-a-team-license]] question.

## Key Details

- A license grants rights to use the work; the publisher keeps copyright.
- Proprietary licenses restrict copying, redistribution and modification; free and open-source licenses grant them under conditions.
- Many publishers prefer selling licenses to selling copies because licenses allow stricter redistribution terms; proprietary licenses often limit use to one computer.
- Caveat: the article does not discuss seat-based counting, and nothing here covers design assets or Figma libraries. Per-seat terms are my own model ([[per-seat-licensing]]).

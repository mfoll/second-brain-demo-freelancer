# Three-Option Proposals

A proposal that offers three scopes at three prices instead of one. The client's question changes from "yes or no?" to "which one?", and the argument moves from my rate to the scope.

## How the options are built

- **A, the first step.** Smallest useful piece, often a [[paid-discovery-phase]] or an audit. Low risk for the client, still profitable for me.
- **B, recommended.** What I think they need. Marked as recommended.
- **C, the extra.** B plus the thing I'd most like to add: a second test round, a design-system handover, a training session.

Each option has different scope, not a discount on the same scope. Each lists what it excludes.

## What I've seen

- Ledgerleaf ($14,000 / $24,000 / $36,000) and Copperline phase 2 both chose B ([[ledgerleaf-proposal-2025-12]], [[copperline-statement-of-work]]). Tomás says clients choose the middle about two times in three.
- C is rarely bought but makes B look reasonable. That is anchoring, described in [[wikipedia-anchoring-effect]] and the related [[wikipedia-decoy-effect|decoy effect]].
- The one time I skipped options, Velora, I lost to a competitor whose offer was effectively option A ([[velora-feedback-call-2026-06-03]]).

## Limits

Overkill for small fixed-scope work, where a day-rate quote is clearer ([[day-rates-suit-small-fixed-work]]). Template and steps: [[proposal-template-2026]], [[write-a-three-option-proposal]].

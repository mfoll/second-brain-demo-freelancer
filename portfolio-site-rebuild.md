# Portfolio Site Rebuild

Replacing my 2024 portfolio, which is a gallery of screens, with a smaller site built around three case studies that show what the research found and what changed for the client. Started because Velora said my case studies showed "screens, not outcomes".

## Outcome

Portfolio relaunched with three case studies that show research evidence and outcomes, not only screens. Target 2026-11-30.

## Current State

- Content plan done ([[portfolio-site-content-plan]]): home, three case studies, services, about, contact. Seven pages instead of the old fourteen.
- Ledgerleaf case study approved by [[wen-zhao]] on 2026-07-28 and written ([[ledgerleaf-case-study-draft]]). Thistlewood approved by [[odette-fairweather]] on 2026-08-19 and written ([[thistlewood-case-study-draft]]).
- Copperline: draft written with placeholders, publication not approved ([[copperline-case-study-draft]], [[will-copperline-approve-a-case-study]]). Fallback is Crumbhollow if there is no answer by 2026-11-15.
- Yvonne's review on 2026-09-10 cut both finished case studies to about 900 words and moved the numbers to the top ([[portfolio-review-with-yvonne-2026-09-10]]).
- Build: static site, hand-coded templates, about 60% done. Hosting stays where it is.

## Scope and Boundaries

The public site and its content. No confidential client material: every screen, number and quote is cleared with the client in writing and logged in [[testimonial-quotes-collection]]. Research participants appear only as counts and pseudonyms (P1 to P12). The proposal template's "What changed for past clients" page reuses the same approved numbers ([[proposal-template-rework]]).

## Working Context

The old site ([[old-portfolio-site-2024]]) was built in a weekend after I left Orchard & Vane. It led with big mockups and had no result on any page. Its analytics ([[portfolio-site-analytics-2026]]) show people did look at it, about 310 visits a month, but only 4 contact form inquiries in the first half of 2026, and two of those were job applications.

The case study shape follows [[evidence-led-case-studies]]: the problem in the client's words, what I did and why, what we found (with the method and sample size), what changed, and what I would do differently. Screens come last and are small. [[wikipedia-case-study]] was a useful reminder that a case study is an argument from one case, not a showcase.

Why only three: Velora's panel read two case studies each. Three good ones beat eight thin ones, and three is what I can keep current. The choice is recorded in [[case-study-selection-2026]].

Rejected: a template site builder, because I want the site to double as a small accessibility showcase and I'd spend the saved time fighting the template; and a case study on Lintel Kit, which belongs on the Gumroad page, not in a client portfolio.

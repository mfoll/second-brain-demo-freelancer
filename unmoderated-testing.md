# Unmoderated Testing

Usability testing where participants do the tasks alone, recorded by a testing tool, with written instructions and no moderator. Cheap per session and fast to field, and unforgiving of a badly written task.

## When I would use it

- Short, well-defined tasks on something already built or a clickable prototype.
- When I need more participants than I can moderate (8 to 20) or a spread of time zones and schedules.
- As a check between moderated rounds, not instead of them.

## What I learned on Lintel Kit

From the [[lintel-docs-unmoderated-test-2026-08-24|getting-started test]]:

- The task text is the moderator. A task that names something that does not exist ("the color guidance page") cannot be rescued mid-session.
- Pilot with one person first, always. [[lorna-beckett|Lorna]] said so and I skipped it.
- People rush. Median session was 14 minutes for three tasks; some barely read the instructions.
- Recordings with voice are worth it even when people forget to talk. Mouse hesitation shows the problem.

## Not for

Exploratory questions, sensitive financial or personal tasks (Copperline would never allow member data in a third-party tool), and screen reader testing, which needs a moderator.

# Diary Study

A research method where participants record their own experiences over days or weeks, in short entries prompted by the researcher. It shows what happens over time and in real context, which a one-hour test cannot.

## What makes one work

- Two prompts a day at most. My first plan had five; [[lorna-beckett|Lorna]] cut it.
- Prompts about what happened, not what they think: "What did you try to do today?" beats "How do you feel about the kit?"
- A kick-off call to set expectations and an exit interview to ask about entries that were unclear.
- Expect entries to fall off in the middle. Mine went from 6 participants on day 1 to 3 on day 8.
- An incentive that arrives at the end, not the start.

## What it found that nothing else did

On Lintel Kit: four of six participants had built their own workaround for dark mode by duplicating the color variables, which never came up in support emails. That was the strongest single piece of evidence for V2's dark mode ([[lintel-diary-study-findings]]).

## Cost

About 20 hours for 6 participants over 10 days: planning 6, running and reminders 5, synthesis 9. More than a usability test with the same number of people.

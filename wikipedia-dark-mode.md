# Dark mode

## Summary

Covers dark mode, also called a dark theme or light-on-dark color scheme: interfaces that show light text and elements on a dark background, now offered as an option by many websites and operating systems. The article discusses its history, claimed benefits such as less eye strain and lower power use on OLED screens, and conflicting views on readability.

## Why It Matters

Starting reference for the dark-mode half of [[lintel-kit-v2]], read in the first week of the project. The useful reminder was that dark mode is a user preference, so the kit should switch modes on the same semantic tokens, not ship a second palette ([[lintel-v2-dark-mode-token-plan]]). Contrast has to be checked in both modes, which is what [[lintel-v2-contrast-pairs-table]] does.

## Key Details

- The history section runs from high-contrast themes in desktop operating systems to system-wide dark settings in the late 2010s.
- Claimed benefits are visual appeal, less eye strain for some users, and power savings on OLED screens; conventional LCD screens may not save power.
- Readability opinions conflict, and forcing dark colors onto pages designed for white backgrounds can produce visual artifacts. That last point is the argument for designing dark mode into the tokens.
- Caveat: the article is general and does not give contrast thresholds; those come from WCAG ([[color-contrast-for-text]]).

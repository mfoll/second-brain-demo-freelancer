# Anchoring effect

## Summary

The anchoring effect is the tendency for a first number or piece of information to pull later judgments towards it, even when people know the anchor is arbitrary. The article covers the experiments behind the idea, proposed explanations, and where anchoring shows up, including negotiation and pricing.

## Why It Matters

Explains both sides of my pricing experience. In a [[three-option-proposals|three-option proposal]] the expensive option C anchors the client and makes option B look reasonable. In the [[velora-pitch]], my single $74,000 price was the only anchor, and Orchard & Vane's $38,000 discovery looked cheap next to it.

## Key Details

- The first number mentioned tends to dominate a negotiation.
- Experts are affected too, not only novices.

## Caveats

Lab findings vary in size and some have replicated weakly. I use it as a reason to order options deliberately, not as a sales trick.

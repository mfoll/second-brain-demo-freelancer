# Value-based pricing

## Summary

The article describes value-based pricing as setting a price from the value a buyer perceives in a good or service, often framed as willingness to pay, rather than from what it costs to produce. Its example is a painting priced far above the cost of canvas and paint because of what owning it means to the buyer.

## Why It Matters

The general idea behind my [[pricing-decision-2025-11-20]]. I read an earlier version during the [[pricing-model-review]] and re-read it in August 2026 when writing up [[value-based-pricing]] after the partial reversal.

## Key Details

- Value is estimated from the customer's side, so the seller has to understand the customer's situation first. For me that is the discovery call ([[qualify-a-new-lead]]).
- The approach depends on the buyer actually perceiving the value. If they can't, the price looks arbitrary.

## Caveats

General marketing article, mostly about products. It says nothing about professional services or small fixed-scope work, which is where my own limits came from ([[day-rates-suit-small-fixed-work]]).

# Contract Terms Update

Rewrite my services agreement so I stop financing clients' cash flow: a deposit before work starts, shorter payment terms and a late fee I can actually point to.

## Outcome

Standard services agreement revised with a 50% deposit, net-15 terms and a 1.5% monthly late fee, reviewed by a contracts attorney. Reached on 2025-06-27.

## Current State

- Completed. The [[standard-services-agreement]] is the only template I send. The old [[services-agreement-2024]] is archived.
- Every engagement since July 2025 has used it: Copperline phase 1, Ledgerleaf, the two workshops and Tallowmere.
- One negotiated exception: Copperline phase 2 is net 30 in exchange for monthly milestone invoices ([[copperline-statement-of-work]]).
- No late fee has been charged yet. Ledgerleaf paid one milestone four days late in February 2026 and I let it go.

## Scope and Boundaries

Terms of payment, deposits, late fees, IP transfer, termination and liability. Pricing itself (day rates, value-based options) is under [[pricing-model-review]]. Lintel Kit's buyer license is a different document ([[lintel-kit-license-terms]]).

## Working Context

The trigger was Crumbhollow. The final $5,850 invoice went out on 2025-04-28 on net 30 and was paid on 2025-07-04, 67 days later, after three reminders ([[crumbhollow-final-payment-2025-07-04]], [[crumbhollow-payment-reminders]]). In May and June I was also waiting for the Thistlewood fee to start, so for about five weeks I paid my own bills from savings. My 2024 agreement, adapted from an Orchard & Vane template, had net 30, no deposit and no late fee, so there was nothing to escalate with.

What I changed, and why:

- **50% deposit before kickoff.** Not invoiced on signature but due before the first working day. A client who will not pay a deposit is telling me something ([[deposits-protect-the-relationship]]).
- **Net 15 on all other invoices.** Thirty days was a habit, not a need; small clients pay faster when the date is close.
- **1.5% monthly late fee** on balances more than 15 days overdue. [[nathaniel-brook]] advised a monthly rate stated as a percentage rather than a flat fee, and to say it applies "from the due date" ([[wikipedia-late-fee]] for the general idea).
- **IP transfers on final payment.** Clients get a license to use drafts during the project; ownership moves when the last invoice is paid. This is the lever that made the late fee unnecessary so far.
- **Termination:** either side with 14 days' notice; the client pays for work done to the termination date, and the deposit covers the first half of that.

Rejected: a 100% upfront fee for small projects (scared off Thistlewood in a test conversation), and a kill fee on top of the termination clause (Nathaniel thought it would not survive a dispute and would irritate procurement teams).

Nathaniel's review cost a flat $1,200 ([[contract-review-with-nathaniel-brook-2025-06-20]], [[contract-review-notes-nathaniel-brook-2025-06]]).

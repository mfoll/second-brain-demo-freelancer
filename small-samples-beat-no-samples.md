# Small Samples Beat No Samples

## Stance

Testing with five to twelve people is worth doing on every project, even when a statistician would sniff at the numbers. A small test reported honestly beats a design argued from opinion.

## Scope

Usability testing and short questionnaires on client and product work. Not market sizing, pricing research or anything where a client will treat a percentage as a forecast.

## Why I Think This

- Copperline: 12 participants at baseline, 10 on the prototype. SUS 58 to 76 and transfers 5 of 12 to 9 of 10. Nobody on the board asked for more participants; they asked when it would launch ([[copperline-usability-test-2026-05-21]]).
- The payee search failure (3 of 10) would have been missed by any amount of internal review. Three people is enough to know something is broken.
- Velora's feedback said my case studies lacked research outcomes. Small numbers would have been enough to fix that.

## What Would Change My Mind

A client decision that went wrong because a small sample misled us. Or a result where 10 participants pointed one way and a larger study the other way on the same task.

## Implications

Always report the range and the sample size with the number ([[score-a-sus-questionnaire]]). Never quote a percentage from fewer than 20 people; say "9 of 10". Budget at least one test round into every proposal over 10 days.
